Three things we check that most people miss.

Every phishing email has tells. We automate what security analysts do manually.

  • Is the sender actually who they claim? We verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication records and recover the real sender IP. Fake senders get caught here.
  • Where do the links actually go? Typosquats like "paypa1.com" (L instead of 1) and homoglyph attacks are common. We catch them and check against threat intelligence (VirusTotal, URLhaus, AbuseIPDB).
  • Is there a real person behind this? Legitimate companies use shortcodes and verified domains. Scammers use unregistered domains and generic gateways. We score the entire chain.

Questions people ask.

How do I get the raw email?
Gmail: Open the email → ⋮ (menu) → "Show original" → copy everything.
Outlook: Open the email → ⋯ (menu) → "View" → "View message source" → copy everything.
Paste it into our scanner. That's it.
Do you actually check against threat intel?
Yes. We use VirusTotal (free tier), AbuseIPDB, URLhaus, and RDAP for domain age. All lookups are optional—run fully offline if you prefer.
What if I just want heuristics?
Check the "offline" box. We'll still catch typosquats, homoglyphs, suspicious TLDs, and auth failures. No internet calls needed.
What doesn't it do?
We don't visit links (so we don't see the actual landing page). We don't open attachments. We don't run behavioral sandboxes. For those, use a tool like Any.run or Joe Sandbox.
How is this different from my email provider's spam filter?
Your email provider catches obvious spam. We catch sophisticated phishing that already landed in your inbox—things that look legitimate but aren't. Your provider says "blocked." We say "why."
